# Systems thinking

Map the loops, then find the leverage points (Meadows 1999).

## Map
1. **Stocks:** what accumulates (users, trust, debt, skills, content).
2. **Flows:** what fills and drains each stock.
3. **Loops:** reinforcing loops (more leads to more) and balancing loops (a goal pulls back), each with its
   delay. Name every link's direction and say where it comes from: a tested insight, a source (note and page) or
   "assumed".
4. **Goal and rules:** what the system is actually optimised for, and the rules that make it so, which may differ
   from what it says it is for.

## Leverage
Place candidate interventions on Meadows' list, from shallow to deep, and group them as parameters, feedbacks,
design and intent (Abson et al. 2017):
- **Parameters:** numbers, sizes of buffers, stocks and flows. Most attention goes here, with little leverage
  (Meadows 1999, p. 8).
- **Feedbacks:** delays, balancing and reinforcing loops.
- **Design:** information flows (who sees what), rules, the power to change the structure.
- **Intent:** goals, the mindset the system comes from, the ability to step out of it. Most leverage and most
  resistance (Meadows 1999, p. 21).

For each candidate say: the level, the loop it acts on, what it would change downstream (levels interact: Abson et
al. 2017), and the counterintuitive check: is the obvious intervention pushing in the wrong direction? (Meadows
1999, p. 3)

Each intervention is a concept and goes through `playbooks/experiment.md`. In a complex system, the experiment is a
small probe whose effect is watched, not a full change.
