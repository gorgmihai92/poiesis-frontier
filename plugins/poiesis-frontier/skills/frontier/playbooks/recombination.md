# Recombination

Build a concept as an ordinary base with one unusual pairing in it. Not a pile of unusual parts, and not one more
ordinary variant.

## Read first
Notes in `research/notes` of the plugin's repository:
- `uzzi-2013-atypical-combinations`, read in full (without its supplement). In 17.9 million scientific papers,
  the ones most often among the top 5% by citations had a very conventional bulk of references and a few
  pairings rarely seen together: 9.11 in 100, against 5 in 100 for all papers, and against 5.33 for papers with
  unusual pairings on an unconventional bulk. It is a pattern in which papers were cited, found by observation.
  It is not a test of a way to have ideas, and nothing in it is about design. **Using it as a recipe is this
  plugin's analogy, and it is untested.**
- `yilmaz-2016-design-heuristics`, abstract only. It reports 77 ways product designers vary a concept. They are
  not named in the abstract, so none is used here.
- `chan-2015-near-far-inspiration` (the authors' summary, not the paper) and `dow-2010-parallel-prototyping`
  (read in full): closer sources went with more creative ideas in one study, and how varied a person's set was
  did not predict how good the result was in another. Unusual is not better by itself.

## Build
1. **The ordinary base.** Write the solution most people in this field would build, from the project's research
   notes or the user's description. It is the base, not the thing to beat.
2. **Its parts.** List what the base is made of: the mechanism, the material, who acts, when, where, what
   changes hands, what is shown.
3. **One unusual pairing.** Replace or pair one part with something this field rarely draws on. Where to find
   it: an analogy from `playbooks/lateral.md`, a cell of a design space, a research note from another section of
   the project, a part of another concept in the same note.
4. **Keep the rest ordinary.** One unusual pairing per concept. A concept with several is split into several
   concepts, each with one, so that a test can tell which pairing did the work.
5. **Say which is which.** **Method:** `recombination (base: …; unusual: … from …)`.
6. **Unusual by a check, not by feel.** That a pairing is rare is a claim about what exists. Either
   [poiesis-research](https://github.com/gorgmihai92/poiesis-research), `prior-art`, looked for the nearest work
   and the concept says what was found, or the concept says "prior art not checked". The word "new" is not used.

## Out
- Each concept states its base, its one unusual pairing and where the pairing comes from.
- The assumption is usually in the pairing: that what the borrowed part does where it comes from, it also does
  here. That is what `playbooks/experiment.md` tests.
- Report how many concepts were built and how many are distinct (`rules.md`, "Generating").
