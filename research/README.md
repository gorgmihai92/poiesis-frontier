# Sources behind the rules

The sources behind the rules (each rule names its source in `skills/frontier/rules.md` and the playbooks). One
note per source in `notes/`; texts in `papers/` stay local (none here is open-licence). Each note says what was
read: the full text, a web page, or only the abstract.

- **What kind of problem it is:** [Kurtz & Snowden 2003](notes/kurtz-2003-cynefin.md), the Cynefin framework
  (abstract); [van Beurden et al. 2011](notes/vanbeurden-2011-cynefin.md), the domains and probe, sense, respond.
- **Leverage points:** [Meadows 1999](notes/meadows-1999-leverage-points.md), the twelve places to intervene;
  [Abson et al. 2017](notes/abson-2017-leverage-points.md), four groups, and leverage points interact.
- **Analogies:** [Gentner 1983](notes/gentner-1983-structure-mapping.md), relations, not attributes;
  [Gick & Holyoak 1980](notes/gick-1980-analogical-problem-solving.md), analogies used mostly when prompted
  (abstract); [Chan et al. 2015](notes/chan-2015-near-far-inspiration.md), closer sources gave more creative ideas
  (authors' summary; to read in full).
- **What a thing is for:** [Norman 1999](notes/norman-1999-affordance-conventions.md), real and perceived
  affordances, conventions.
- **Speculative design:** [Auger 2013](notes/auger-2013-speculative-design.md), the perceptual bridge (abstract);
  [Phadnis et al. 2015](notes/phadnis-2015-scenario-planning.md), several scenarios, not one.
- **Convincing is not evidence:** [Tversky & Kahneman 1983](notes/tversky-1983-conjunction-fallacy.md), the
  conjunction fallacy (reference only).
- **Experiments fixed in advance:** [Nosek et al. 2018](notes/nosek-2018-preregistration.md), prediction and
  postdiction, preregistration.

**Methods used, sources not yet read:** de Bono (lateral thinking: provocation, reversal), Dunne & Raby 2013
(*Speculative Everything*). They are used as methods; no rule rests on a claim from them.
