# The ideation–execution gap: execution outcomes of LLM-generated versus human research ideas (Si, Hashimoto & Yang, 2025)

- **Source:** arXiv preprint 2506.20803, version 1 of 25 June 2025 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803 (pages =
  PDF pages)
- **File:** `../papers/si-2025-ideation-execution-gap.pdf`
- **Licence:** arXiv preprint; licence not checked: kept local.
- **Evidence:** empirical · a blinded randomised trial, registered in advance: 43 researchers each carried out
  one idea assigned at random, 19 of them written by researchers and 24 generated by a model; 58 reviewers
  wrote 181 reviews of the finished projects (p. 3, 5).
- **Read:** part of the text: the abstract and the opening (p. 1–2), the study design (p. 3), recruitment
  (p. 5), the quantitative results (p. 6–7) and the limitations (p. 12), on 2026-11-22. Not read: section 5 on
  why the gap arises, the examples, and the appendices (p. 13–131).

## What it says
- The ideas are those of the 2024 study. Each executor had three months and spent about a hundred hours; each
  project was then written up in four pages and reviewed without the reviewers knowing where the idea came from
  (p. 1, 3, 5).
- **Scores fell after execution, and fell more for the model's ideas.** On a ten-point scale the model's ideas
  lost 1.05 on novelty, 1.76 on excitement, 1.88 on effectiveness and 1.98 overall. The researchers' ideas
  moved by −0.01, +0.08, −0.05 and −0.63. The difference between the two drops is significant on all four
  (p from 0.001 to 0.025 after correction) (p. 7).
- Before execution the model's ideas scored higher on all four measures. After execution the order was
  reversed, but with 43 projects that reversed difference is not statistically significant (p. 7).
- The authors say they lack the statistical power to confirm their registered hypothesis that the two kinds of
  idea differ in their scores after execution (p. 6).
- How an idea scored on paper said little about how it scored once carried out: the correlation was weak in
  most cases (p. 7).
- Limits stated: all ideas were prompting techniques, chosen to be doable in three months; 43 is a modest
  sample, sensitive to who the executors were; replication is needed (p. 12).

## What it means for the plugin
- Support for the existing rule "ranked by the test, not the appeal": an idea's rating before it is tried is a
  poor guide to what it is worth after, and in this study the gap was larger for generated ideas.
- The study does not show that generated ideas are worse once executed: that comparison was not significant.
  The rule says what the study found, a bigger drop, and no more.
- One field, one kind of idea, 43 projects, one preprint. A reason to test before ranking, not a rate.

## Quotes
- "a good idea should not simply appear to be novel, it should also result in better research after being executed" (p. 1)
- "we do not have sufficient statistical power to directly confirm our pre-registered hypothesis" (p. 6)
- "our final sample size of N = 43 executed projects remains modest" (p. 12)
