# Leverage points for sustainability transformation (Abson, Fischer, Leventon, Newig, Schomerus, Vilsmaier et al., 2017)

- **Source:** Ambio 46(1): 30–39 · https://doi.org/10.1007/s13280-016-0800-y
- **File:** none; read on PubMed Central (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5226895/), so claims cite
  sections, not pages.
- **Licence:** CC BY 4.0 (open access).
- **Evidence:** analytical (a research agenda built on Meadows 1999).

## What it says
- Meadows' twelve points can be grouped into four kinds of system characteristics, from shallowest to deepest:
  parameters, feedbacks, design and intent ("Leverage points").
- Sustainability research and policy have mostly addressed shallow leverage points ("Leverage points").
- Deep leverage points may be harder to change but can lead to transformational change; leverage points are not
  independent, and interventions in one realm have effects on the others.

## What it means for the plugin
- The four groups make Meadows' list easier to use: the systems playbook groups candidates as parameters,
  feedbacks, design, intent, and asks what each changes downstream.

## Quotes
- "Different leverage points are not independent."
