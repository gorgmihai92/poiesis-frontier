## What it says
- A speculative enactment has participants act inside a speculation, in circumstances where something matters
  to them: in the authors' projects, a real date, or their own wedding story in print (p. 5386, 5391).
- Showing people a fiction and collecting what they say studies how people react to and talk about a future.
  The authors want what people do when part of it is staged around them (p. 5392).
- They do not claim to give anyone an experience of the future ahead of time. Any picture of the future carries
  present concerns projected forward (p. 5392).
- **Participants are in on it.** There is no intention to deceive, and an enactment should not depend on tricks
  behind the scenes. People know who the researchers are and that it is research, give informed consent, and
  can ask or withdraw at any point (p. 5387, 5392, 5394).
- Staging uses a routine people already know (a date, meeting a supplier), which keeps the speculation ordinary
  and checks wilder utopias and dystopias (p. 5393).
- Thirteen practical guidelines, among them: research the domain first; pilot the materials with colleagues and
  ask whether the setup makes sense to them; recruit selectively and say up front what taking part involves;
  plan your own role; decide what is recorded and whether recording spoils the interaction; debrief people
  afterwards, off-stage; take care with how the work is presented to the media, where it can be misunderstood
  (p. 5394–5395).
- Limits stated: enactments are effortful, small and one-off; when a cheaper fiction, such as an imaginary
  abstract, is enough remains to be worked out (p. 5395).

## What it means for the plugin
- The staging step of the speculative playbook: choose a familiar routine as the scene, pilot the materials,
  tell people before they agree that the thing is fiction and what taking part involves, decide what is
  recorded, and debrief afterwards.
- What people do and say in a staging is data about the present: their reactions to this artefact, today. It is
  recorded as that, never as how the future will be.
- A methods paper from the authors' own practice, read in part. It supplies a procedure and its ethics; it does
  not show that enactments produce better knowledge than showing an artefact and asking.
