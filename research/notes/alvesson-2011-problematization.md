## What it says
- The usual way to a research question is to find a gap in what exists. That leaves the assumptions under the
  literature untouched. Problematization identifies and challenges those assumptions and builds the question on
  the challenge (p. 1, 5–6).
- The target is what is implicit: the key move is to turn what is commonly taken as truth or fact into an
  assumption (p. 10).
- **Five kinds of assumption**, from narrow to broad (p. 8–9):
  - *in-house:* shared inside one school of thought;
  - *root metaphor:* the image the subject is seen through;
  - *paradigm:* what exists, what counts as knowledge, which methods count;
  - *ideology:* political, moral and gender-related;
  - *field:* shared even by schools that oppose each other.
- Broader challenges can have more impact and can also be superficial; a sharp challenge to a narrow assumption
  can matter as much (p. 9).
- **Six principles**, iterative and explicitly not a recipe (p. 10): choose a domain of literature; identify
  and state the assumptions under it; evaluate them; develop an alternative ground; consider it in relation to
  its audience; evaluate the alternative.
- Reading is narrow and deep: a path-defining study, one or a few authoritative summaries, or a few recent
  influential pieces (p. 10).
- Tactics for finding each kind (p. 10–11): follow a school's internal debates; name the metaphor and produce a
  rival one; read from another world view without settling in it; take a position with very different interests
  and values, or treat something seen as negative as possibly positive; look for what opposing camps never
  debate, and look at other fields.
- Not every assumption is worth challenging: ask what challenging it could open. Assumptions can seldom be
  tested directly (p. 12).
- The alternative should not be a ready-made counter-position borrowed from a rival school (p. 12).
- **The test the authors propose is interest, not truth.** An alternative that confirms everything the audience
  assumes is obvious; one that denies everything is absurd; one that accepts some and denies some is
  interesting. They add: try it on people from the audience (p. 13).

## What it means for the plugin
- The first step of the speculative-research playbook: take one or two key texts, write down what they treat as
  fact under each of the five kinds, pick the assumption whose challenge would open most, and write the
  alternative as a question.
- Being interesting is the paper's criterion for a question worth asking. It is not evidence that the
  alternative is true: in this plugin the question still goes to a protocol written before the data, and a
  challenged assumption stays an assumption.
- A method from management studies, by its proposers, read in part. The illustration was not read, so the
  playbook does not copy it.
