# Atypical combinations and scientific impact (Uzzi, Mukherjee, Stringer & Jones, 2013)

- **Source:** Science 342(6157): 468–472 · https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1240474 (pages = PDF pages of the
  author's copy, which has a cover page; add 466 for the journal's printed page)
- **File:** `../papers/uzzi-2013-atypical-combinations-scientific-impact.pdf` (an author's copy, from
  kellogg.northwestern.edu)
- **Licence:** © AAAS 2013; the copy is marked for personal, non-commercial use: local only.
- **Evidence:** empirical, observational · 17.9 million research articles in the Web of Science (p. 2). No
  experiment: it relates how a paper combines earlier work to how often it was cited later.
- **Read:** full text, p. 2–6, on 2026-11-22. Not read: the supplementary materials, where the method's details
  and the checks by field and decade are.

## What it says
- For each paper, every pair of journals cited together was scored against chance: pairs seen more often than
  chance are conventional, pairs seen less often are atypical (p. 2–3).
- Two numbers describe a paper: the middle of its pairs (how conventional the bulk is) and its most unusual
  tenth (whether it has any atypical pairs) (p. 3).
- Science is mostly conventional. Fewer than 4% of papers had a bulk of pairs rarer than chance (p. 3).
- Papers in the top 5% by citations, eight years on, were: 9.11 in 100 among papers with a conventional bulk
  **and** some atypical pairs; 5.82 with a conventional bulk only; 5.33 with atypical pairs only; 2.05 with
  neither. The background rate is 5 in 100. Papers published 1990 to 2000 (p. 4).
- Impact peaked when the bulk was extremely conventional (the 85th to 95th percentile), with or without
  atypical pairs (p. 5).
- Papers by three or more authors had atypical pairs more often than solo papers (49.7% against 36.1%) (p. 4).
- The authors carry the idea outside science only as examples (e-books that keep the page-turn, jeans with a
  watch pocket), and say the balance "may be" critical there too (p. 5).

## What it means for the plugin
- The recombination playbook builds a concept as an ordinary base plus one unusual pairing, not as a pile of
  unusual parts and not as one more ordinary variant.
- What the paper measured: citations of scientific papers, and pairings of journals. It is a pattern in what got
  cited, not a test of a way to have ideas, and nothing in it is about design concepts. Using it as a recipe for
  generating is this plugin's analogy, marked as such in the playbook.
- Observational: papers were not assigned to be conventional or atypical. Something else could produce both the
  mix and the citations.

## Quotes
- "The highest-impact science is primarily grounded in exceptionally conventional combinations of prior work yet simultaneously features an intrusion of unusual combinations." (p. 2)
- "novelty and conventionality are not opposing factors in the production of science" (p. 4)
