# Do the best design ideas (really) come from conceptually distant sources of inspiration? (Chan, Dow & Schunn, 2015)

- **Source:** Design Studies 36: 31–58 (online 2014) · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2014.08.001
- **File:** none; the author copy (https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/Chan-Dow-Schun-desstudies-nearfar-inpress.pdf)
  could not be fetched. Read: the authors' summary as reported by their lab page. Pages unconfirmed; to read in full.
- **Licence:** closed.
- **Evidence:** empirical (hundreds of concepts across about a dozen design challenges on an online innovation
  platform that records which sources each concept builds on).

## What it says
- Building on sources went with more creative ideas, but conceptually closer sources were more beneficial than far
  ones, and this held across design problems (authors' summary).

## What it means for the plugin
- Far analogies are not assumed to be better. The lateral playbook uses them to break a frame and always adds near
  ones.
- One platform, one kind of rating: a strong lead, not a law; to read in full before relying on it more.
