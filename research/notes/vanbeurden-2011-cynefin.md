# Making sense in a complex landscape: how the Cynefin Framework from Complex Adaptive Systems Theory can inform health promotion practice (van Beurden, Kia, Zask, Dietrich & Rose, 2011)

- **Source:** Health Promotion International 28(1): 73–83 · https://doi.org/10.1093/heapro/dar089
- **File:** none; read on the publisher's page, so claims cite sections, not pages.
- **Licence:** open to read on the publisher's site; licence not stated: local only.
- **Evidence:** analytical (framework applied to practice, with examples).

## What it says
- Simple: cause and effect mostly linear and agreed; sense, categorise, respond.
- Complicated: stable cause and effect that needs research to understand; sense, analyse, respond.
- Complex: non-linear, many agents, patterns understood only in retrospect; develop probes to reveal emergent
  patterns, sense which help, and respond by amplifying and resourcing them.
- Chaos: no visible cause and effect; act, sense, respond.
- Disorder: not known which domain applies.
- The default approach, reducing a system to its parts, is inadequate where issues have many interacting causes
  (abstract).

## What it means for the plugin
- The frame playbook's table: frontier belongs in the complex domain, where experiments are several small probes
  and what works is amplified.
