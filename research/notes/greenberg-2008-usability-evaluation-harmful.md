# Usability evaluation considered harmful (some of the time) (Greenberg & Buxton, 2008)

- **Evidence:** analytical · an essay by two long-standing practitioners of usability evaluation, argued from

- **Read:** full text, p. 1–9, on 2026-11-22. Not read: the reference list (p. 10).

## What it says
- The thesis, stated three times: the choice of evaluation method, if any, has to come from the problem or
  question at hand and fit it (p. 1, 2, 9).
- Done during early design, usability evaluation can silence ideas that do not fit today's interface norms
  (p. 1).
- An early design is a sketch: it shows the core of an idea and has holes. Testing a sketch as if it were a
  prototype finds problems that can kill it outright, above all when a new design is compared with a
  conservative one (p. 4–5).
- Testing one early sketch also pulls the team into repairing it, "getting the design right", before they have
  compared several ideas, "getting the right design". Usability evaluation belongs after several ideas were
  generated, criticised and considered (p. 5).
- The usual methods look for problems and say little about benefits. Costs are easier to measure than benefits,
  and that tilts decisions too early (p. 5).
- Usable is not useful. Many successful things were useful first and became usable later (p. 6).
- A test built to show one case where the new thing wins is an existence proof, and weak science. A stronger
  test tries to refute the claim (p. 3). The authors accept existence proofs for really new things and ask for
  risky tests on variations of established ideas (p. 8).
- What to use early instead: a critique of the design (why it is so, what else was considered, expected
  problems, prior art), a design rationale, scenarios of use, case studies. They add that this is no licence for
  "hand-waving and slick demos" (p. 8).
- Dropping usability testing altogether would be just as wrong; in many cases it is the right method (p. 9).

## What it means for the plugin
- The experiment playbook asks what stage the concept is at and matches the test to it. A sketch is criticised
  and compared with other sketches; a usability test waits for a prototype of a concept that was chosen.
- At the sketch stage the kill criterion is about the concept's assumption and its benefit, not about usability
  problems, which a sketch always has.
- The paper asks for tests that try to refute. That is this plugin's own rule for every stage: stage decides the
  kind of test, never whether there is one.
- An essay, not a study: it gives reasons and examples. Nobody measured, here, how many good ideas early
  usability tests kill.

## Quotes
- "premature usability evaluation of early designs can eliminate promising ideas or the pursuit of multiple competing ideas" (p. 2)
- "Usability evaluation should happen after multiple ideas are generated, critiqued, and considered." (p. 5)
- "the more the test tries to refute the hypothesis, the more powerful it is" (p. 3)
