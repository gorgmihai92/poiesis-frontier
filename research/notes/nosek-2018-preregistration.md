## What it says
- Science generates hypotheses from existing observations (postdiction) and tests them with new ones (prediction);
  mistaking one for the other reduces the credibility of findings (abstract).
- Hindsight bias makes outcomes seem more predictable after the fact, so a postdiction feels like a prediction.
- Preregistration is committing to the analysis plan before observing the outcomes (abstract).
- Exploratory work is not discouraged: data can be used to open new questions, if reported as such.
- Analyses that are not in the plan inform postdiction: they generate explanations to test later
  ("Preregistration distinguishes prediction and postdiction").
- The authors call the case for preregistration strong in principle and backed by some correlational evidence;
  they say there is not yet enough experimental evidence that it makes findings more reproducible (same
  section).
- Plans change. A change made before the outcomes are seen is documented; a change made after is reported with
  its reason. Either is better than no plan (challenge 1).
- When the data already exist, ask who has seen them and what was passed on. What was known in advance is
  written down (challenge 3).
- Work with few expectations in advance is legitimate, as long as what it finds is used to generate hypotheses
  and not claimed as evidence for them. A first study, mostly exploratory, can supply the predictions for a
  second. Where data are hard to collect, there is no shortcut: progress is slower (challenge 7).
- A report that dwells on the two interesting results out of ten analyses overstates them, even when every
  analysis was planned (challenge 9).

## What it means for the plugin
- Frontier's concepts are generated, not tested; the experiment is the test. The kill criterion is written and dated
  before the experiment runs, and what the experiment shows but did not predict is reported as exploratory.
- The speculative-research playbook writes the protocol before any data exist, records changes with their date
  and reason, and reports every planned comparison, not only the striking ones.
- For studies with a small group the playbook takes challenge 7 as it stands: a first round is exploratory and
  says so, and its findings are leads for a second round with predictions.

## Quotes
- "define the research questions and analysis plan before observing the research outcomes" (abstract)
- "The rules of statistical inference have no empathy for how hard it is to acquire the data." (challenge 7)
