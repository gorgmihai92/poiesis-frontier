# The preregistration revolution (Nosek, Ebersole, DeHaven & Mellor, 2018)

- **Source:** Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 115(11): 2600–2606 · https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114
- **File:** none; read on PubMed Central (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856500/), so claims cite the
  abstract and sections, not pages.
- **Licence:** open to read (PNAS, PMC); not CC: local only.
- **Evidence:** analytical (a methods argument drawing on the empirical literature).

## What it says
- Science generates hypotheses from existing observations (postdiction) and tests them with new ones (prediction);
  mistaking one for the other reduces the credibility of findings (abstract).
- Hindsight bias makes outcomes seem more predictable after the fact, so a postdiction feels like a prediction.
- Preregistration is committing to the analysis plan before observing the outcomes (abstract).
- Exploratory work is not discouraged: data can be used to open new questions, if reported as such.

## What it means for the plugin
- Frontier's concepts are generated, not tested; the experiment is the test. The kill criterion is written and dated
  before the experiment runs, and what the experiment shows but did not predict is reported as exploratory.

## Quotes
- "define the research questions and analysis plan before observing the research outcomes" (abstract)
