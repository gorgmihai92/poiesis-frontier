# Extensional versus Intuitive Reasoning: The Conjunction Fallacy in Probability Judgment (Tversky & Kahneman, 1983)

## What it says (from general knowledge, unverified)
- People judge a conjunction of events ("A and B") as more probable than one of its parts ("A"), although it can
  never be: a detailed, representative story feels more likely than a bare one ("Linda" problem).

## What it means for the plugin
- Detail adds plausibility, not probability. A scenario or concept is not made more convincing with invented
  specifics; each added detail is one more assumption.
