# Structure-mapping: A theoretical framework for analogy (Gentner, 1983)

- **Source:** Cognitive Science 7(2): 155–170 · https://doi.org/10.1016/s0364-0213(83)80009-3 (pages = PDF pages)
- **File:** `../papers/gentner-1983-structure-mapping.pdf`
- **Licence:** free to read on the publisher's site, not open-licence: local only.
- **Evidence:** analytical (a theory of analogy, with examples).

## What it says
- In an analogy, relations between objects are mapped from base to target, while attributes of the objects are not;
  in literal similarity both are (p. 1, 3).
- A comparison that shares object attributes but not relations is a "mere appearance" match (p. 4).
- Systematicity: people prefer to map connected systems of relations, governed by higher-order relations, over
  isolated facts (p. 6).

## What it means for the plugin
- The lateral playbook writes the problem's structure as relations, and keeps an analogy only if its relations carry
  over; an analogy that only looks alike is dropped.
