# Interaction relabelling and extreme characters: methods for exploring aesthetic interactions (Djajadiningrat, Gaver & Frens, 2000)

- **Source:** Proceedings of DIS 2000 (Designing Interactive Systems): 66–71 ·
  https://doi.org/10.1145/347642.347664 (pages = the proceedings' printed pages). The third author is printed
  "J.W. Frens"; the DOI's record lists him as "Fres".
- **File:** none; read in the ACM Digital Library's reader.
- **Licence:** © ACM 2000; free to read on the publisher's site, not open-licence: local only.
- **Evidence:** a description of two design techniques by the people who used them, illustrated with one student
  project (an appointment manager). No comparison with another technique and no measure of the ideas produced.
- **Read:** full text, p. 66–71, on 2026-11-22.

## What it says
- **Interaction relabelling:** take an existing product, pretend it is the product to be designed, and tell and
  act out how it works (p. 66–67). Forcing a mapping between two things with different functions moves attention
  from the function to the possible actions (p. 67).
- The two products need not be related; the authors say unrelated ones "may lead to more creative ideas" (p. 67).
- Mechanical products with many moving parts worked well for them; so did doing it in a group, and handling the
  object instead of only thinking it through (p. 67).
- The mapping need not be consistent, and the result need not look like the object that was relabelled (p. 67).
- **Extreme characters:** in place of a typical member of a target group, design for fictional characters with
  exaggerated emotional attitudes. Extremes expose traits that are common but stay hidden because they are
  antisocial or clash with a person's status (p. 67).
- In the project, three characters gave three concepts. The authors judged two of the three stereotypical, in
  look and in role; the third was developed further, and some of its ideas carried over to ordinary uses
  (p. 70).
- The choice of character matters. Two of the three were stereotypes, "to at least one reviewer offensively so";
  the authors apologise and plan less clichéd characters (p. 71).
- The characters are a device inside the design process. The authors do not present them as real users, or as
  something to carry into the final design (p. 71).

## What it means for the plugin
- Two operators in the estrangement playbook: relabel an unrelated object's actions as the thing being designed,
  and design for a character with an exaggerated attitude.
- Both come with the paper's own caution: an extreme character slides into a stereotype. The playbook asks for
  characters defined by an attitude to the task, not by a group of people, and keeps the character out of the
  concept that is reported.
- One project, described by its authors. The paper shows what the techniques are and what went wrong with one of
  them. It does not show that they produce better ideas than brainstorming, although it says so in passing.

## Quotes
- "It is the resulting richness of gestures, not the metaphor that is important." (p. 67)
- "the extremes make one realize that the way things are is not necessarily the only way" (p. 71)
