- **Source:** Proceedings of DIS 2014: 375–384 · https://doi.org/10.1145/2598510.2598522 (pages = PDF pages of

- **File:** `../papers/pierce-2014-counterfunctional-things.pdf` (University of California eScholarship,

- **Licence:** the repository copy is under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (stated on its cover page): kept local.
- **Evidence:** design research · the authors' own design experiments with digital cameras, and interviews of
  about an hour with 8 people aged 19 to 56, who saw 10 concepts, 5 of them as physical prototypes, in a lab
  (p. 6). Prototypes were prompts for conversation, not products to evaluate.
- **Read:** full text, p. 2–11, on 2026-11-22. Not read: the reference list.

## What it says
- A counterfunctional thing counters some of its own essential function and is still recognisably that thing
  (p. 4). The paper's claim: designing around an extreme limit on function can be a source of new, positive
  possibilities (p. 2).
- The process, which the authors decline to turn into a prescriptive method, has three lines: normally one can
  …; now one cannot …; but now one might … (p. 4).
- Opposing a familiar function makes a thing strange in one of three ways: bizarre, problematic (it prompts
  questions) or useful (p. 3).
- People's reactions varied widely. One camera that hides its pictures until it is broken open was the favourite
  of 4 of the 8; five wanted to use it (p. 7). One whose pictures decay while viewed was called useless by all 8,
  although it appealed to the designers as an idea (p. 8).
- Three things to watch in such a design: whether it is interesting to use or only interesting to think about;
  whether new features added next to the removal hide the removal; and whether it recalls an older technology,
  which made a limit easier to accept (p. 9–10).
- What people said were first reactions in a lab to partly working prototypes. How the things would be used can
  only be answered by a longer field study (p. 6–7).

## What it means for the plugin
- One operator in the estrangement playbook: remove or reverse a function the thing is known for, then write what
  becomes possible. The three lines are quoted from the paper as a prompt, not as a method it endorses.
- A design the designers found appealing as an idea was the one every participant rejected. A concept from this
  operator goes to people early, and "interesting to think about" is recorded apart from "would use".
- Eight people, one medium (cameras), first reactions. It shows the operator produces concepts some people want,
  not how often.

## Quotes
- "Designing digital technologies around extreme functional limitations can be a valuable source of new positive possibilities." (p. 2)
- "what appealed to us conceptually as design researchers did not resonate with our participants" (p. 8)
