## What it says
- A design fiction tries to look "not obviously untrue" while its core is untrue on purpose. The authors answer
  their title's question with yes: the search for plausibility can, and often does, lead to deception (p. 13).
- Three groups (p. 3–7):
  - **obviously fiction:** the artefact carries visible signs of it, such as props that could not be products;
  - **identified as fiction:** labelled by its makers, and sometimes still taken for real;
  - **ambiguous or concealed:** not labelled, or presented as real.
- A label can fail. The authors' own fictional research paper carried "design fiction" as a keyword and said so
  in its conclusion; its reviewers, and people at the conference, did not fully grasp that it was fiction (p. 5).
- What decides whether people are deceived is the format and how familiar it is. A fiction that copies a known
  form (a research paper, a crowdfunding video, a user manual) is read as that form (p. 6, 14).
- A concealed case: two designers told a reporter that an imagined tooth implant was heading for production,
  after an earlier reporter lost interest on hearing it was only an idea. The coverage that followed treated it
  as a real invention (p. 7).
- In their own project the authors posted videos with nothing marking them as fiction, and decided against a
  fake crowdfunding page as deliberately deceitful. One viewer took the device for real and was hard to
  persuade otherwise (p. 12).
- Deliberate deception by designers is rare, they conclude; ambiguity is common, even in work that is labelled
  (p. 14).

## What it means for the plugin
- The speculative playbook's last step: the fiction is stated in the artefact itself, where it cannot be cropped
  away, and said aloud before and after a session. A keyword or a closing line is not enough.
- The more familiar the format an artefact borrows, the stronger the marking has to be. An artefact in the form
  of a research paper, a news item or a product page is the risky case.
- Nothing fictional is released in public unmarked, and nobody is told it is real, whatever attention that would
  bring. In this plugin a public release is only ever proposed to the user.
- A reflective paper built on examples. It shows that labelled fiction has been taken for real, not how often.
