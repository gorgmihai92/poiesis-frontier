# Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System (Meadows, 1999)

- **Source:** The Sustainability Institute, Hartland VT, 1999 · https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/ (pages = PDF pages)
- **File:** `../papers/meadows-1999-leverage-points.pdf`
- **Licence:** © The Sustainability Institute, 1999; freely available from the Donella Meadows Project: local only.
- **Evidence:** analytical (a practitioner's synthesis of system dynamics; the author calls the list tentative).

## What it says
- Twelve places to intervene, from least to most effective: constants and parameters; buffer sizes; the structure
  of stocks and flows; delays; balancing loops; reinforcing loops; information flows; rules; the power to change
  the system's structure; goals; the paradigm; the power to transcend paradigms (p. 5).
- People often find the leverage point by intuition and then push it in the wrong direction (Forrester's story,
  p. 3).
- Most attention goes to parameters, but there is not much leverage in them (p. 8).
- The list is tentative and its order "slithery"; the higher the leverage point, the more the system resists
  changing it (p. 21).

## What it means for the plugin
- The systems playbook places each intervention on this list and checks whether the obvious one pushes the wrong
  way.
- Deep interventions are proposed with their resistance in mind, and tested by small probes.

## Quotes
- "Everyone is trying very hard to push it in the wrong direction!" (p. 3)
- "The higher the leverage point, the more the system will resist changing it" (p. 21)
